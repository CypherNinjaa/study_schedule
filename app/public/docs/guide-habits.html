Habits

Small, repeatable actions that compound. Track daily, weekly, or custom habits — including "inverse" habits you want to avoid — with streaks, completion rates, and a heatmap.

What a habit is here

A habit is something you intend to do (or avoid) on a schedule. Unlike a task, it doesn't disappear when done — it comes back on its next due day so you can build a streak. Examples the app seeds for you include Study, Wake up early, Sleep on time, Exercise, Read, Code, Revision, No excessive social media, and Drink water.

Frequencies

FrequencyDue…
Dailyevery day
Weeklya set number of times per week (e.g. "Read 4×/week")
Customon the specific days you choose

The app only expects a habit on the days it's actually due, so your completion rate and streaks are fair.

Inverse habits

Some habits are about not doing something — like "No excessive social media." These inverse habits count as complete when you successfully avoid the behaviour for the day. They're a gentle way to keep distractions in check without a separate system.

Inverse habits pair naturally with the distraction factor in your productivity score and the social-media insight on Analytics — together they help you notice and curb time sinks.

Streaks, rates, and the heatmap

For each habit you'll see:

  • 🔥 Current streak — consecutive due-days completed.
  • 🏆 Best streak — your all-time record for that habit.
  • Completion % — how reliably you've kept it over a window.
  • Calendar heatmap — a GitHub-style grid showing which days you kept it.

Completing every habit that's due on a given day also advances your overall habits streak (one of the six streaks) and fills your Habits ring on the dashboard.

XP from habits

Keeping a habit for the day earns +10 XP, granted once per habit per day. As with everything else, you can't farm it by toggling. See XP & levels.

Creating and managing habits

  1. Add a habit

    Give it a name, choose its frequency, and (for inverse habits) mark it as something to avoid.

  2. Check it off daily

    Tap to mark today done. The streak and heatmap update immediately.

  3. Archive when it's no longer relevant

    Archiving keeps your history intact without cluttering the active list — nothing is deleted.

Start with fewer than you think

Three to five habits you actually keep beats fifteen you ignore. If a habit keeps slipping, the app suggests shrinking it until it feels easy — a smaller habit kept is worth more than a big one skipped.
