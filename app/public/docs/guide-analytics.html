Analytics & insights

Where your logged effort becomes understanding. Charts show how your study, tasks, habits, and focus trend over time, and plain-language insights point out what's working and what needs attention.

The charts

Analytics visualises the data you've been building day by day:

  • Study time trends — daily and weekly hours, so you can see consistency (or gaps) at a glance.
  • Subject distribution — where your hours actually go, which often surprises people.
  • Study methods — how much time is active recall and practice versus passive reading.
  • Task completion — how many you planned versus finished, by category.
  • Habit consistency — completion rates and streaks over time.
  • Productivity score trend — your daily score plotted across days.
  • Focus & distraction — Pomodoro focus time against distraction signals.

Automatic insights

Beyond charts, the app reads your data and surfaces short, specific observations — the kind a good study coach would point out:

Insight typeExample
Exam proximity"Time Series Analysis exam in 9 days — 4 topics still open."
Neglected subject"No study time on Computational Quantitative Techniques for 2 weeks."
Best study time"You focus best in the morning — your longest sessions start before noon."
Streak milestones"7-day study streak — your best this month."
Distraction alert"Social-media time up this week compared to last."
Balance"Coding is strong; exercise has slipped — worth a short session today."

Insights are generated entirely on your device from your own logged data. Nothing is sent anywhere to produce them — see Privacy & data safety.

How to read your analytics

  1. Look for trends, not single days

    One quiet day means nothing; a two-week slide means something. Watch the direction the lines move.

  2. Check the balance

    If one subject or one activity dominates, decide whether that's intentional or drift.

  3. Act on one insight

    Pick the single most useful observation and turn it into a task or goal — that's analytics doing its job.

Accuracy in, insight out

Analytics is only as honest as your logging. The more truthfully you record study, tasks, and habits, the sharper — and more trustworthy — these insights become.
